# Deadline Survival Guide — Presentation Script (3 min 40 s)

**Topic:** Task management for university students (Dump → Sort → Block)
**Goal:** Audience leaves with a 3-step system they can start *tonight*.
**Pace:** about 125 words/min. The spoken script is ~415 words (about 3:20 of talking), plus ~20 seconds of pauses for the show of hands, the "scrolling" question and slide clicks = about 3:40.
**Rapport (see board):** use "you / we / me too", smile, eye contact, one show of hands.

## Slide 1 — Title (0:00–0:25)

[Walk to the front. Smile. Wait 2 seconds until the room is quiet.]

Good morning, everyone! Before we start, one rule: please keep your questions until the end, and I promise I'll answer every one.

Quick show of hands. [Raise your own hand.] Who here has ever said, "I'll start tonight," and then suddenly it's 2 a.m. and you've watched three episodes instead? [Pause. Smile at the hands.] Yes, me too!

Today I'll show you three steps to survive deadlines, with no panic.

## Slide 2 — The deadline trap (0:25–0:55)

[Click. Point at the three quotes.]

Here's the thing. Most of us don't fail because we're lazy. We fail because we have no *system*.

Our brain is juggling the essay, the group project, the exam, and the laundry, all at once. And a brain that is juggling can't focus.

So don't try harder. Work smarter, with three steps: **Dump. Sort. Block.**

## Slide 3 — Step 1 · DUMP (0:55–1:30)

[Click. Hold up one finger.]

Step one: **Dump**. Take five minutes and write down *everything* you need to do. Big, small, boring, scary. Notes app, paper, anything. [Point at the sticky notes.] "Essay due Friday." "Email my professor." Even "do laundry." Every single thing goes on the list.

Why? Because your brain is great at having ideas, but terrible at storing them. Once it's on the list, your head is free.

Try it tonight. You'll feel lighter instantly.

## Slide 4 — Step 2 · SORT (1:30–2:25)

[Click. Hold up two fingers. Point at each box as you name it.]

Step two: **Sort**. This is the Eisenhower Matrix, a simple four-box grid. For every task, ask two questions: *Is it urgent? Is it important?*

Urgent and important? **Do it now**, like the essay due tomorrow.

Important but not urgent? **Schedule it**, like studying for the midterm. This is where winners live.

Urgent but not important, like a quick reply in the group chat? **Hand it off**, or do it fast.

And neither? [Look at the audience.] Let me ask you: is scrolling TikTok urgent? Important? [Pause. Let them answer.] Exactly. **Delete.**

## Slide 5 — Step 3 · BLOCK (2:25–3:05)

[Click. Hold up three fingers. Slow down on the first line.]

Step three: **Block**. A task without a time is just a wish.

So give every task a slot in your calendar, and use the Pomodoro trick: 25 minutes of full focus, phone away, then a 5-minute break. After four rounds, take a longer one. [Sweep your hand along the timeline.] For example, take "study for the midterm" from your Schedule box and give it a start time: Tuesday, 7 p.m.

Short sprints beat long, painful marathons. And breaks are part of the plan, not a reward for finishing.

## Slide 6 — Recap + challenge (3:05–3:40)

[Click. Slow down. Look at the whole room.]

So remember: **Dump it. Sort it. Block it.**

Here's your challenge. Tonight, take just five minutes: dump your tasks, pick your top three, and block time for the first one. That's it.

A small system makes a big change. And your future self, the one with no 2 a.m. panic, will thank you.

Thank you! [Smile. Pause.] I'd be happy to take your questions.

[Click to Slide 7.]

## Q&A cheat sheet

**For every good question, use this 4-step pattern:**
**Thank** → **Comment** → **Answer** → **Check**

### 1. Good question
> **Q:** "What if everything feels urgent?"
> **Thank/Comment:** "Thank you, that's a very good question. I'm glad you asked."
> **Answer:** "Then ask one more question: *what happens if I do this tomorrow?* If the answer is 'nothing bad,' it's not truly urgent. Start with the item with the real consequence or the closest deadline, and the rest can wait."
> **Check:** "Does that help?"

### 2. Difficult question
> **Q:** "Doesn't Pomodoro fail if I'm already in the flow?"
> **Answer:** "Good point. If you're deeply focused, keep going. The timer is a tool, not a rule. Use it to *start*, and ignore it when you're on a roll. Take your break when the flow ends."
> If you don't know: "That's a great question and I don't have the full answer. Let me find out and email you after class."

### 3. Unnecessary question (already covered)
> **Q:** "Sorry, how long is one Pomodoro?"
> **Answer:** Stay kind. Never say "I already said that." → "Thanks for checking! 25 minutes of focus, then a 5-minute break. Does that make sense?"

### 4. Irrelevant question
> **Q:** "Which phone do you use?"
> **Answer:** "Thanks for asking, but that's a bit off-topic. Let's chat about it after class so we can stay on task management."

## If something goes wrong (technical issues and heckling)

**Technical issues:** Stay calm. *Politely notify* whoever is responsible. Do **not** blame anyone. Fix it yourself only if it takes 1–2 minutes. *Buy some time* with a joke or an anecdote.

| Problem | What to do |
|---------|-----------|
| Slides won't open / format breaks | Open the PDF copy (`Deadline_Survival_Guide.pdf`) from your phone or USB. Or present from the **board**: draw the 2×2 matrix, which takes 20 seconds. |
| Projector / laptop fails | Present without slides. You know the 3 words: Dump, Sort, Block. Use the board and the printed cue cards. |
| Clicker / pointer fails | Click with the keyboard (→ key) and point with your hand. |
| Microphone fails | Move closer, speak up, tell the back row to raise a hand if they can't hear. |
| Internet / power cut | Nothing in this deck needs internet. Keep going from the printed script. |

*Buy-time joke:* "While we wait for the tech to catch up, here's a deadline survival tip: technology failing five minutes before a presentation is also a deadline problem."

**Heckling:** You set the rule at the start ("questions at the end"). Stay calm. Do **not** engage or argue. Say: "Great point. Let's talk about it after the presentation." Use light humor. If it continues, ask your teacher to help.
